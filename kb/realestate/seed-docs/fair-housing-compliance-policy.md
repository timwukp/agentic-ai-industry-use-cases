# Fair Housing Compliance Policy (Demo Brokerage)

Document ID: POL-FH-001 | Effective: 2026-01-01 | Owner: Compliance Office

## Protected classes

Under the federal Fair Housing Act (and broader state/local law in many markets),
it is illegal to discriminate based on: race, color, national origin, religion, sex
(including sexual orientation and gender identity), familial status, or disability.
Several states add: age, marital status, source of income, military status.

## Rules for valuation and analysis work

- Never use the demographic composition of a neighborhood as a value factor or
  present it as a reason a property is worth more or less.
- Demographics may be reported as neutral statistics (income, density, growth) when
  a client asks for market data, but must not be framed as desirability judgments
  tied to protected classes.
- School quality may be discussed via published ratings only; do not proxy protected
  characteristics through school commentary.
- Crime statistics: report official figures neutrally; do not use them to steer.

## Steering prohibition

Steering — guiding clients toward or away from neighborhoods based on protected
characteristics — is prohibited even when the client requests it. Standard response:
provide objective data for ALL areas matching the client's stated non-protected
criteria (price, size, commute, features).

## Advertising and listing language

- Describe the PROPERTY, not the ideal occupant. "Walk-in closets" is fine;
  "perfect for young professionals" is not.
- HUD-flagged phrases (e.g., "exclusive neighborhood", "family-friendly building",
  "adult building" outside legal senior housing) must not appear in listings.

## Appraisal bias safeguards

- Value conclusions must trace to property and market factors documented in the
  workfile; reviewer spot-checks 10% of reports quarterly for unsupported gaps
  versus comparable neighborhoods.
- A client challenge alleging bias triggers a second independent valuation and a
  compliance review within 10 business days.

## Violations

Report suspected violations to the Compliance Office within 24 hours. Confirmed
violations result in mandatory retraining and may result in termination and
regulatory referral.
